# Argentine National Motorsport — Getting an Engineering Foot in the Door

**For:** Ing. Joaquín Buttini — Ingeniero Electromecánico (UTN FRSR), San Rafael, Mendoza. Fully mobile. Willing to accept **lower pay in exchange for real race-engineering experience.**
**Compiled:** July 2026. **Honesty note:** Argentine teams almost never post public vacancies — hiring is informal, by contact and reputation. This document is therefore a **realistic map of teams, roles, hubs and the pathway in**, not a list of live job ads. No vacancy below is invented; where nothing is publicly open, it says so.

---

## TL;DR — the three most realistic entry points

1. **Get the credential that opens the network: ACTC Educación + the UTN "Especialización en Automóviles de Competición" (Córdoba / FDC Academia).** This posgrado is aimed *exclusively at graduate engineers*, is UTN-accredited, and includes hands-on practice **inside a TC2000 team workshop (FDC Motor Sport) and the Oreste Berta engine shop**. For someone with a strong engineering base but zero motorsport CV, this is the single most direct, legitimate door — and it is built for exactly Joaquín's profile.
2. **Target the Córdoba hub directly** (Córdoba capital + Alta Gracia + Río Cuarto). This is the engineering heart of Argentine motorsport: FDC Motor Sport, Oreste Berta S.A., Toyota Gazoo Racing's build shop, and dozens of rally/TC preparadores. Relocating here (Joaquín is mobile) multiplies his odds far more than staying in Mendoza.
3. **Enter through a "school" category as a junior data/telemetry engineer** (Fórmula Nacional / Turismo Nacional / TC Pista / Top Race Junior). Smaller budgets, more churn, more willingness to take an unproven-but-capable young engineer who will work cheap to learn.

There are **no public engineering job postings** on the teams' or ACTC's sites, LinkedIn, or category pages as of this research — consistent with how the sector hires (word-of-mouth). What **is** genuinely open and enrollable right now:

Pre-registration cohorts open periodically. → posgrados.frc.utn.edu.ar/especializacionCompeticion and **fdcacademia.com**. Director: **Javier Ciabattari**.
- **ACTC Educación — "Diplomatura Mecánico Especialista"** (more mechanic-oriented, UTN Gral. Pacheco) — useful if he wants the hands-on shop credential too.

These are the concrete, real things Joaquín can *act on this month*. They double as the network — the instructors are working race engineers and the courses are run by the bodies that do the hiring.

## Recommended "way in" for a young electromechanical engineer

**Phase 0 — position the profile (free, now).** Reframe his CV for motorsport: structural calculation, SolidWorks/Inventor/AutoCAD, hydraulic/electrical design, PLC/automation, manufacturing documentation (ASME/API), the thesis machine design, the wind-tunnel *experimental validation* work (design→build→test is exactly the race-engineering loop), and honest sim-racing enthusiasm (Assetto Corsa/ACC) framed as motivation, **not** professional experience. Data-acquisition literacy (AiM, MoTeC, Bosch, Pi/Cosworth) is the language of these roles — start self-studying it.

**Phase 1 — get inside the network via ACTC Educación (low cost, remote-friendly).** Enroll in the intensive track (virtual is offered for Mendoza-distance). It is cheap, UTN-certified, and puts him in a room (even virtual) with working TC engineers. Immediate credibility signal on a CV that currently has none in the sport.

**Phase 2 — commit to the UTN "Especialización en Automóviles de Competición."** This is the strongest single move: a graduate-engineer-only posgrado whose *practices happen inside a TC2000 workshop and the Oreste Berta engine shop*. That practice is the audition. It converts "electromechanical engineer who likes cars" into "engineer the shop already knows."

**Phase 3 — be physically near the work: Córdoba.** Argentine motorsport engineering is concentrated in Córdoba (capital + Alta Gracia + Río Cuarto): Oreste Berta S.A., FDC Motor Sport, Toyota Gazoo's build operation, and the rally ecosystem. Joaquín is fully mobile — relocating (or doing the posgrado on-site practices) here is worth more than any online application. Buenos Aires province (ACTC in CABA; Toyota's Zárate simulator) is the secondary hub.

**Phase 4 — take the cheap, junior, "school-category" seat.** The realistic first paid/entry role is **junior data/telemetry engineer** in Fórmula Nacional, Turismo Nacional, TC Pista, or Top Race Junior — not a Súper TC2000 lead-engineer chair. These are where budgets are thin and a capable young engineer willing to work weekends for experience is welcome. Offer exactly that: "I'll do the data grunt-work to learn." His stated willingness to accept lower pay is a genuine advantage here.

**Phase 5 — leverage the engine/design shops as a parallel track.** Berta and FDC hire on *design, machining, measurement and dyno* skills — precisely Joaquín's engineering core. A CAD/design or dyno/test-engineer role there is a legitimate, less-glamorous back door into the paddock that pays and builds motorsport-specific credibility.

**On approach method:** these teams respond to a person, not a portal. Warm intro > email > cold call. The courses generate the warm intros. Secondarily, show up at rounds (San Juan's El Villicum and Córdoba circuits are near-ish to Mendoza), and volunteer for a race weekend to get a foot in — informal help is how many mechanics/engineers actually start.

**Reality check.** Pay in national motorsport is low relative to oil & gas; the honest play is to treat the first 1–2 years as paid (or barely-paid) apprenticeship that builds a motorsport CV — which also directly strengthens his parallel UK Motorsport-Engineering ambition. Keeping the Tassaroli income while doing the (largely virtual) UTN specialization is a sensible hedge before any full relocation.
