## Visa / work-rights reality per region

- **UK (most leads):** Non-UK hires need a **Skilled Worker visa**; the employer must be a licensed sponsor. Large firms (Prodrive, Ricardo, Multimatic) can sponsor; smaller teams may not — always confirm on the posting. **Cleanest route:** enrol in the UK Motorsport Engineering postgrad, then use the post-study **Graduate visa** to work 2 years without needing sponsorship up front, converting to Skilled Worker once employed. This aligns with his stated plan and is his single strongest lever.
- **USA (IMSA, Ilmor US, NASCAR/IndyCar teams):** H-1B is **rare in motorsport** and lottery-gated. Teams typically use **O-1 (extraordinary ability)** or **P-1 (specific event)** visas — both hard for someone without an established motorsport record. Treat US as a later-stage target, most reachable via internal transfer from a UK/global employer.
- **Australia (Supercars):** Requires a skilled work visa (e.g. TSS/482 or skilled-independent). Engineers do appear on skilled-occupation lists at times; more attainable than the US for a direct hire, but still needs employer/visa sponsorship. English-speaking, relocation-friendly.
- **Canada (Multimatic North America):** Generally more accessible skilled-immigration pathways than the US; worth targeting Multimatic's Canadian sites specifically.
