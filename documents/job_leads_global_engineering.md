## Highlights — where to actually spend effort

1. **Global service companies (SLB, Halliburton, Weatherford, Baker Hughes, NOV) are his single best door.** They (a) explicitly hire 0–2 yr engineers of any discipline, (b) sponsor visas and relocate globally as a matter of routine, (c) reward exactly what he has — mobility, adaptability, field/maintenance grounding — and (d) route people into the Gulf and other high-income tax-free regions. **SLB Field Engineer Trainee is the flagship.**
2. **Saudi Aramco "Engineering Graduates (<3 yrs experience)" is a rare direct-apply, sponsored, entry-friendly play at a supermajor.** His ~2 yrs fits the bracket; tax-free package. Apply directly at careers.aramco.com (no agents/intermediaries).
3. **Income reality:** entry-level expat engineer in the Gulf ≈ **$60k–$90k tax-free + housing/allowances**; mid-level $100k–$150k; rotational field/contractor roles can pay more once he has offshore certs. This is a large step up from an Argentine salary and matches his maximize-income goal.
4. **His Tassaroli narrative is a genuine asset:** manufacturing O&G equipment for **Halliburton, Titan, Petrobras** under **ASME/API** — name-drop this to Halliburton and to service-company recruiters; it's real, verifiable supply-chain adjacency.
5. **Certifications gap to close for offshore:** **OPITO BOSIET / HUET** (offshore survival) is the standard ticket for North Sea/Guyana/Australia offshore. Getting C1 English certified (his Nov 2026 Oxford exam) also strengthens every application.

## Realistic assessment — reachable NOW vs after more experience

**Reachable NOW (apply this quarter):**
- **SLB, Weatherford, Halliburton, Baker Hughes field-engineer / early-careers tracks** — best odds, real sponsorship, high ceiling. Top priority.
- **Saudi Aramco Engineering Graduates (<3 yrs)** — direct-apply, sponsored, tax-free; competitive but he's in-bracket.
- **NOV field service** — realistic, slightly more selective.
- **Canada Express Entry pool** — start the PR profile now in parallel (he qualifies on experience + degree); it's a slow-burn but high-value long game.

**Reachable AFTER ~1–3 more years / added certs:**
- **ADNOC / QatarEnergy national-oil-company grad or engineer schemes** — usually want 3–5 yrs or nationals; easier to enter the Gulf first via a service company, then move.
- **ExxonMobil Guyana** — local-content rules favor Guyanese; foreigners mostly experienced/specialist.
- **North Sea (UK/Norway)** — hardest immigration wall for a non-EU entry-level hire; needs a sponsor-licensed employer + offshore certs.
- **Australia LNG** — SID-visa sponsorship skews mid-level; better with more experience or via skilled-migration PR.

**Bottom line:** the fastest, most credible route to a high-income international role for him **right now** is a **global oilfield-services field-engineer position (SLB first)**, with **Saudi Aramco's graduate track** as a strong parallel direct-apply, and a **Canada Express Entry profile** opened as a long-game PR play. The Middle-East NOCs, Guyana, North Sea and Australia become realistic once he adds ~1–3 years and offshore survival certs (OPITO BOSIET) and lands the C1 certificate.
